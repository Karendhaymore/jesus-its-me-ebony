Dear Jesus... ♡

Sometimes you just need somebody to talk to.

Dear Jesus,

So... apparently growing up is supposed to be exciting.

Nobody mentioned the part where everything starts changing at the same time.

We moved from Chicago to Denver. I have a new school, new friends, family stuff, boy stuff, and about a million questions nobody seems to answer the same way twice.

And then there are the questions about You.

I don’t know if I’m supposed to ask all the things I want to ask.

But I’m going to anyway.

It’s me, Ebony. ♡

About the Book

Ebony Reed’s world is changing fast.

A move from Chicago to Denver means a new home, a new school, new friendships, and a whole new set of challenges.

As Ebony navigates friendship drama, boys, her changing body, colorism, an absent father, family, faith, race, and the pressure to fit in, she turns to the one person she believes is always listening: Jesus.

Her prayers aren’t perfect.
They’re honest.
And sometimes messy.

Faith doesn’t mean never having questions. ♡

Hi. I’m Ebony.

I’m eleven years old, which means everybody expects me to act grown when it’s convenient and then reminds me I’m a kid when it’s not.

Make that make sense.

I used to live in Chicago. Now I’m in Denver, starting over at a new school and trying to figure out new friends, family, boys, my body, my faith—and basically life.

I notice things. I think about things. And I ask questions. Lots of them.

Sometimes I ask my family. Sometimes I ask myself. And sometimes, when I really need to talk... I talk to Jesus.

I’m not sure I have everything figured out yet. Actually, I’m pretty sure I don’t.

But I’m learning that maybe I don’t have to.

Being eleven is enough. ♡

Bring Ebony Into the Classroom

A relatable story. Meaningful conversations. A voice young readers can recognize.

Jesus, It’s Me, Ebony gives young readers something every child deserves to encounter in literature: permission to wonder, question, feel, and find their own voice.

Through Ebony's experiences, students encounter age-appropriate opportunities to discuss friendship, family, identity, faith, race, colorism, growing up, belonging, self-esteem, and navigating change.

Ebony doesn't have all the answers. She gives young readers a character they can think with, laugh with, question with, and, perhaps most importantly, recognize themselves in.
